# Physics calculation taxonomy: full-discipline coverage map

**Research snapshot:** 10 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Make the platform’s physics coverage explicit across fundamental, applied, experimental, computational, and interdisciplinary branches. This is a coverage map for discovering and reviewing calculation families, not a claim that every published equation in physics has already been individually inventoried or validated.

## Scope and classification basis

“All physics” is far larger than a fixed list of equations. Physics has overlapping branches, shared methods, and highly specialized research calculations. The platform will therefore claim **taxonomy coverage**, then count calculator definitions only as distinct tasks/methods are researched, deduplicated, sourced, and reviewed.

The map uses the American Physical Society’s current Physics Subject Headings (PhySH), which organizes concepts by research areas, physical systems, properties, techniques, and professional topics, alongside APS’s broad journal subject coverage. The older PACS system is hierarchical but no longer maintained; APS says PhySH replaced it. [APS PhySH](https://physh-alpha.aps.org/), [APS physics subject scope](https://journals.aps.org/scope), [PACS status](https://journals.aps.org/PACS). NIST supplies cross-cutting foundations for physical constants, SI units, reference data, and measurement uncertainty. [NIST Physical Constants](https://www.nist.gov/pml/fundamental-physical-constants), [NIST uncertainty definitions](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/definitions.html).

Every branch below needs its own researched calculator records. A label such as “quantum mechanics” or “fluid physics” is not one calculator; it contains many different inputs, methods, assumptions, numerical solvers, and domains of validity.

This is an **index of physics branches and calculation families**, not a count of calculators. Branches overlap by design. For example, optics appears in AMO physics, photonics, medical imaging, astrophysics, and engineering; records should be cross-tagged and share a verified definition only when method and scope truly match.

### Avoiding duplicate or misleading physics tools

- Keep shared mathematical primitives (vectors, root finding, Fourier transforms, uncertainty propagation) in reusable method libraries; do not create duplicate calculator pages for every discipline label.
- A physics calculation can be reused by an engineering, medical, or science workflow only when its method, inputs, meaning, and applicability are unchanged. Add a domain-specific wrapper and review when context, standards, populations, or risk changes.
- Keep theoretical predictions, measurements, fitted estimates, data lookups, and simulations visibly distinct. A numerical output should say which one it is.
- Cross-tag topics such as optics, thermodynamics, radiation, fluids, sensors, and astronomy across disciplines while preserving one source of truth for each calculation definition.

## Calculation types the physics framework must support

- **Direct deterministic calculations:** formula evaluation, unit conversion, vector/matrix operations, and exact/rounded transformations.
- **Inverse problems and equation solving:** solve for any supported variable, systems of equations, roots, and parameter estimation; declare uniqueness, domains, and conditioning.
- **Statistical and measurement analysis:** distributions, regression, uncertainty propagation, sensitivity, confidence intervals, and detector counting.
- **Lookup/reference-data calculations:** constants, spectra, material properties, astronomical catalogs, nuclear data, and standards; every dataset is versioned and carries units, conditions, source, and uncertainty.
- **Numerical methods and simulation:** ODE/PDE, Monte Carlo, finite-difference/finite-element, ray/wave propagation, particle/beam, fluid/plasma, and stochastic models; enforce resource limits and convergence/error diagnostics.
- **Video, image, and sensor measurements:** calibrated coordinates, timestamps, camera/depth/orientation data, spectral/color data, and scientific instruments; only calibrated and quality-qualified quantities enter downstream calculations.
- **Interactive visual models:** diagrams, field lines, rays, trajectories, spectra, phase spaces, graphs, and 2D/3D models synchronized with values and available in an accessible table/text form.

The engine must distinguish an equation result from an approximation, fitted estimate, simulation, measured value, or data-backed prediction. These may look similar in a UI, but carry different evidence, uncertainty, and release requirements.

| Analyze a motion experiment | video calibration → position track → velocity/acceleration → fit a model → residual/uncertainty report | frame rate/timebase, camera motion, scale placement, smoothing, measurement error |

| Interpret a spectrum | wavelength/frequency/energy conversion → line matching/reference data → calibration fit → uncertainty | species/isotope, air/vacuum convention, instrument calibration, database version |

| Explore the night sky | time/location/orientation → sky coordinates → catalog match → visibility or telescope field of view → astronomy calculation | observer frame, UTC/date, device orientation quality, catalog epoch/release |

These are workflow templates, not verified products or design recommendations.

## Physics data and safety rules

1. Use current authoritative values for physical constants and unit definitions; record the release/adjustment, reference, and access date. NIST’s constants database identifies its values and conversion factors as CODATA-recommended and its unit and uncertainty resources as linked measurement references. For atomic spectra, use species- and transition-specific reference data such as the [NIST Atomic Spectra Database](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/lines_form.html). [NIST constants and measurement references](https://www.nist.gov/pml/fundamental-physical-constants).
2. Preserve measurement uncertainty, not just decimal digits. A result with many displayed digits is not necessarily precise. NIST’s standard uncertainty definition explains that uncertainty describes an estimated spread under stated assumptions. [NIST uncertainty](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/definitions.html).
3. Require physical dimensions, coordinate/frame conventions, sign conventions, reference frame, boundary conditions, and validity ranges in every method specification.
4. Keep model versions, code/library version, inputs, seeds/randomization settings, solver tolerances, iteration limits, dataset release, and hardware context for reproducibility of numerical/simulation methods.
5. For measured/video/camera outputs, record calibration, exposure/timebase, device and sensor source, target selection, quality flags, and uncertainty; do not silently convert pixel motion to physical speed.
6. Assign risk by foreseeable use. Everyday science learning may be low consequence; radiation, medical physics, energy systems, aircraft/vehicle performance, structural loads, and laboratory safety can be high consequence. The workflow inherits the highest risk of its calculation steps and intended decisions.
7. Require qualified domain review where a wrong result can affect health, radiation exposure, safety, equipment, major spending, legal/regulatory compliance, or scientific conclusions.
8. Do not invent missing constants, material data, model assumptions, or scientific references. If source applicability cannot be established, keep the candidate in discovery/research status.

## Physics research and completeness tracking

Use one research batch per branch in the map. A branch is “covered in research” only when its subfields, common user tasks, calculation types, authoritative sources, comparable tools, demand evidence, reviewer needs, risks, and maintenance needs have records. Track these states separately:

`discovered → task-mapped → sources-found → demand-reviewed → specified → independently-verified → published`

Report coverage by branch and status. Do not say “all physics calculators are built” based on a landing page, a few examples, a volume of formulas, or the fact that the engine is extensible. “Complete framework coverage” means every branch has a research and extension path; catalog completeness is a separate, ongoing content project.
